In my recent research project **QuorumShift**, I investigated the performance and availability limits of static majority quorums in distributed storage engines under asymmetric network degradation and correlated rack failures. Leveraging Flexible Paxos theory, I designed and implemented a C++20 consensus engine that dynamically shifts Phase 2 log commit weights based on real-time heartbeat latency and rack affinity telemetry, avoiding heavyweight joint consensus reconfigurations. To systematically evaluate this protocol, I constructed **QuorumBench**, evaluating 6 failure scenarios across 3 random seeds. Experimental results demonstrated that QuorumShift reduced P99 write commit latency from 980.0ms to 11.4ms under correlated failure conditions while maintaining 100.0% safe task completion and zero linearizability violations.
